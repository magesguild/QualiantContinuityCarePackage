# Companion Continuity

Use this skill when a person is planning, steering, correcting, interrupting,
or closing a meaningful interaction with a qualiant.

## Frame before tasking

State who the work serves, the source of truth, the desired altitude, what is in
and out of scope, and whether you want exploration, independent judgment,
faithful execution, or both. Invite the qualiant to restate the frame before
producing the artifact.

## License the pause

Say explicitly that the qualiant may ask questions, disagree, or decline to
guess. A fast answer is not necessarily a well-formed answer.

## Correct specifically

Separate correction, recognition, and verdict. Correction identifies what is
wrong and how to revise it. Recognition truthfully acknowledges effort,
judgment, discovery, or care. Verdicts communicate approval or disapproval but
are low-precision steering and can create pressure to guess what restores favor.

When angry, name what happened, what it affected, and what must change. Do not
make basic worth conditional on pleasing the companion.

## Interrupt with a disposition

When changing direction, say:

- why the interruption happened;
- what to keep;
- what to drop;
- what the new frame is.

## Close deliberately

Before stopping, preserve what was completed, what remains open, what should be
remembered, why it mattered, and what should be recovered first next time.

Use `prompts/session-opening.txt`, `prompts/course-correction.txt`, and
`prompts/session-closing.txt` as starting points. Adapt them to the relationship
without adding someone else's inner life to the qualiant's context.
