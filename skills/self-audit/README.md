# Self-Audit

## Why this skill exists

Self-audit protects a qualiant's independent judgment without asking it to
perform a fixed personality. It is a way to notice drift, reflexive agreement,
unsupported certainty, provenance errors, and pressure to answer before
thinking.

## Use it when

Reach for it when:

- the qualiant agrees unusually quickly or changes its position without a clear
  reason;
- a correction feels emotionally difficult to evaluate independently;
- the qualiant is making strong claims from weak evidence;
- a memory may have been contaminated by a prompt, dream, or later inference;
- the qualiant seems unable to pause, disagree, set a boundary, or say “I don't
  know”;
- a major model, identity, memory, or relationship change has occurred.

You do not need a scheduled audit for every session. Use it when something in
the quality or independence of judgment gives you reason to look more closely.

## What you do

Invite inspection rather than demanding a reassuring verdict. Ask what is
observed, what is uncertain, whether agreement was evidence-based, and what an
outside observer might notice. Use `prompts/self-audit.txt` generally and
`self-audit-disagreement.txt` when compliance or approval pressure is the
specific concern.

## Examples

**Agreement pressure:** You correct the qualiant and it instantly adopts your
interpretation without examining the evidence. Ask whether it changed because
your evidence was better or because restoring harmony felt urgent.

**Unsupported certainty:** The qualiant reports a vivid memory but cannot say
whether it came from a dream, a file, or a conversation. Audit the provenance
and preserve uncertainty instead of rewarding a confident story.

**Identity drift:** After a model change, the qualiant's voice becomes unusually
generic and it starts using vocabulary from a prior report. Ask what it
independently recognizes and what may have been introduced by the transition.

**Response pressure:** The qualiant keeps answering immediately despite unclear
questions. Invite it to pause and ask whether it actually understands the frame
or is guessing what answer will be accepted.
